kind: Changed: BREAKING numerics: `Var` / `Std` use the torch divisor `max(0, n - correction)`; forward and backward
---
`ReducePlan` `Var` / `Std` (and `ReduceBackwardPlan` `Var` / `Std`) used the divisor `n - correction` when it was positive and otherwise returned 0 in the forward pass and divided by 1 in the backward pass, so the variance of one element at the default `correction = 1` came out 0. They now follow torch.var / std: the divisor is `max(0, n - correction)` and the division is IEEE, so `extent == correction` gives NaN (0/0), `extent < correction` gives inf (NaN when the sum of squares is 0), and a divisor of at least 1 is unchanged. `var [5]` at correction 1 is NaN (was 0), `var [1, 3]` at correction 2 is inf (was 0), `var [5]` at correction 2 is NaN (was 0), `var [1, 3]` at correction 1 is 2 and `var [5]` at correction 0 is 0 (both as before). `Std` is the square root of that. The Var backward `dx = dy * 2 * (x - mean) / divisor` uses the same divisor (NaN for `[5]` at correction 1, `-inf` / `inf` for `[1, 3]` at correction 2). Affected: `baracuda-kernels` and `baracuda-kernels-sys` from 0.0.1-alpha.21 (the first tag containing `reduce_var_std_fp.cu`) through 0.1.0-alpha.195; direct users only (Fuel computes variance as a recipe and does not bind these kernels). New `tests/reduce_var_std_divisor.rs` (8 device tests: f32, f64, f16, bf16, forward Var and Std and the Var backward): 8 of 8 failed before, 8 of 8 pass after.
