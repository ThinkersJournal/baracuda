kind: Added: `baracuda-cuda-emit` lowers the mbarrier family and `griddepcontrol` (ROADMAP S1 slice 1)
---
`Cuda::lower_kernel` now spells `MbarInit`, `MbarArrive` (with and without `expect_tx`), `MbarCompleteTx`, `MbarWait` (`try_wait.parity` loop), `GridDepWait` and `GridDepLaunchDependents` for sm_90 or newer (`#error` guard plus `KernelInterface::requires`; bad counts and parities trap). `CopyBulk` (1-D `cp.async.bulk` completing on an mbarrier; run, alignment and 16-byte multiple checked) is spelled too. Static `Private` arrays (zeroed per-thread) and `F16`/`Bf16` allocation and buffer elements (storage only: copied, a `Load`/`Store` of one is refused by name) now lower. `MatrixLoad` (`ldmatrix`, sm_75) and `MatrixStore` (`stmatrix`, sm_90) are spelled in a launch whose subgroup is exactly 32. Named barriers (`bar.sync`/`bar.arrive`), the `Cluster` barrier and a cluster launch (`__cluster_dims__`) are spelled (the named-barrier lowering itself shipped as the 0.56.13 fix) `Wgmma*` and `Tcgen05*` stay declined by name. Tests: `tests/sm90_statements.rs` (source shape, born-red against the previous `kernel.rs`, and an NVRTC `sm_90a` assemble with an `sm_80` negative control). Hopper execution is untested (the dev box is sm_89) and the mbarrier phase-parity semantics are the oracle's, not yet cross-checked on hardware (ROADMAP S6). `tests/conformance_device.rs` (features `nvrtc,device-tests`, run under `gpu-run`) runs five kernel-layer kernels on the device and compares every output byte with Unpopped's oracle (ROADMAP S7 slice 1): a guarded global kernel, `Shared` with a block barrier, named barriers, and a `Private` array; first run on an sm_89 device, all agree; Hopper statements are not covered (no device). S7 slice 2 extends the device runner to `CopyAsync` (plain and `valid`) and `ldmatrix` x1/x2/x4 with and without `.trans` (13 kernels, all agree with the oracle on an sm_89 device); a 16-bit integer (`U16`/`I16`) local now lowers as storage only (load, store, widen by `Cast`; arithmetic on it is refused by name), which is what makes a fragment observable.
