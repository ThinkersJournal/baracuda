kind: Added: half-precision (f16, bf16) Gather, ScatterAdd, GatherBackward and IndexSelectBackward, plus the NaN sweep pins (tests; one FA2 backward divergence pinned by name)
---
One PR for the half index family (it is the Fuel GAP-413 set after MaskedFill, alpha.215) and the test-only NaN sweep pins. Out-of-range indices are still skipped (a documented divergence from torch; `IndexBoundsPlan` detects them); half scatter-add and the backward kernels use the compare-and-swap add, so the order of additions into one cell is undefined, as torch CUDA atomics.

**GatherPlan, f16 and bf16 sources.** `GatherPlan` (`out[..., j, ...] = src[..., index[..., j, ...], ...]`) now accepts f16 and bf16 sources, i32 and i64 indices. It routes them onto the existing 2-byte bit-copy kernels (`gather_u16`, `gather_i64idx_u16`); a gather has no arithmetic, so every value comes back bit-for-bit (NaN payloads, signalling NaN, -0, infinities, subnormals, the largest finite values). The index policy is the one f32 has: an out-of-range or negative index leaves that output cell untouched (a kernel cannot raise an error; `IndexBoundsPlan` detects it), pinned by a sentinel in the test. Before, `select` returned `Unsupported` for a half source (ROADMAP E3 #3; Fuel's GAP-413 asks for the half index ops). Device test `gather_half` (f16 and bf16; sm89): gather dim first and last on rank 2, the middle dim on rank 3, duplicate indices, a size-1 source, i32 and i64 indices, out-of-range and negative indices: 8 of 8 pass, red on the old plan (`select` returned `Unsupported`, all 8). `ScatterPlan` already took f16/bf16 (its own kernels); `GatherBackwardPlan` and `ScatterAddPlan` half are separate rows (new atomic family for the latter). The CI device run is queued, not claimed.

**ScatterAddPlan, f16 and bf16.** `ScatterAddPlan` (`out[..., index[..., j, ...], ...] += updates[..., j, ...]`) now accepts f16 and bf16, i32 and i64 indices (ROADMAP E3 #4; Fuel's GAP-413). Four new symbols, `baracuda_kernels_scatter_add_{f16,bf16,i64idx_f16,i64idx_bf16}_run`, instantiate the existing scatter-add kernel on `__half` / `__nv_bfloat16`; the add is the library's compare-and-swap loop on the 32-bit word that holds the 2-byte cell (`baracuda::atomic::add`), not a native half atomic. With duplicate target indices the ORDER of the additions into one cell is undefined, so a half result is only as reproducible as the rounding of its partial sums (the plan docs say so); an out-of-range or negative index is skipped, the policy of every indexing plan (`IndexBoundsPlan` detects it). Device test `scatter_add_half` (f16 and bf16; sm89): 200 updates into 3 rows with exactly representable partial sums, 20 runs each for i32 and i64 indices, equal to an f64 accumulation rounded once to the dtype; `+inf + -inf` and a NaN update give NaN, `+inf` plus a finite value gives `+inf`, an untouched cell keeps its exact bits (including a NaN payload); out-of-range and negative indices are skipped: 6 of 6 pass, red on the old plan (`select` returned `Unsupported`). Not measured: sums whose partial sums are inexact (the order dependence is by design, not asserted), and throughput. The CI device run is queued, not claimed.

**GatherBackwardPlan and IndexSelectBackwardPlan, f16 and bf16.** `GatherBackwardPlan` and `IndexSelectBackwardPlan` (the adjoints of `GatherPlan` and `IndexSelectPlan`: scatter-adds of the upstream gradient into `dsrc`) now accept f16 and bf16, i32 and i64 indices (ROADMAP E3; Fuel's GAP-413). Eight new symbols, `baracuda_kernels_{gather,index_select}_backward_{f16,bf16,i64idx_f16,i64idx_bf16}_run`, instantiate the existing backward kernels on `__half` / `__nv_bfloat16`; the add is the library's compare-and-swap loop on the 32-bit word holding the 2-byte cell. As with half `ScatterAddPlan`, the ORDER of additions into one cell is undefined when indices repeat (torch's CUDA `index_add_` / `scatter_add_` atomics are order-nondeterministic too), so a half result is only as reproducible as the rounding of its partial sums; an out-of-range or negative index is skipped, the shared policy of every indexing plan (`IndexBoundsPlan` detects it). Device test `indexing_backward_half` (f16 and bf16; sm89): 200 gradient rows into 3 `dsrc` rows with exactly representable partial sums (20 runs, i32 and i64 indices) for both plans, equal to an f64 accumulation rounded once to the dtype; a NaN gradient poisons its cell only and an untouched NaN cell keeps its exact bits; out-of-range and negative indices skipped: 8 of 8 pass, red on the old plans (`select` returned `Unsupported`). Not measured: sums whose partial sums are inexact (order-dependent by design, not asserted) and throughput. The CI device run is queued, not claimed.

**NaN sweep pins (tests only, no kernel change), plus one measured divergence.** Test-only; every probe below passed on the unchanged kernels except ONE measured divergence from torch in the vendored FlashAttention-2 backward (last paragraph), which is pinned by name, not fixed.

**Forward and sort pins.** `ssd_chunk_scan_nan` runs `SsdChunkScanPlan` (f32, f16, bf16) on the device with a NaN in `x`, `B`, `C`, `dt` or `A` and asserts the NaN reaches exactly the outputs the recurrence implies (a column from that step on, every column from that step on, one step only, or the head) and no other batch, head or earlier step. Measured on an RTX 4070 (sm89): 18 of 18 pass on the unchanged kernel, so this row of the NaN sweep needed no fix. `selective_scan_nan` does the same for `SelectiveScanPlan` (u, delta, A, B, C, z; with and without softplus and gating; f32, f16, bf16): 21 of 21 pass on the unchanged kernel. `sort_backward_nan` runs `SortPlan` then `SortBackwardPlan` (f32, f64, ascending and descending) on inputs with two NaNs and both infinities and gradients holding NaN, +inf and -inf: NaN sorts last ascending and first descending, the indices are a permutation, and the backward scatter carries every gradient exactly: 4 of 4 pass on the unchanged kernels. `scan_cummax_nan` now also runs cummax and cummin, forward and backward, in f16 and bf16 (16 of 16 pass on the unchanged kernels).

**Backward pins.** `ssd_chunk_scan_backward_nan` compares `SsdChunkScanBackwardPlan` (f32, f16, bf16) with an f64 reverse-mode oracle of the Mamba-2 recurrence written in plain IEEE arithmetic, over a finite control (positive control: values match) and a NaN in dy, x, B, C, dt and A (batch 2, heads 2, 16 steps): the NaN pattern of dx, dB, dC, ddt and dA matches the oracle cell for cell (for example a NaN in A gives 521 NaN cells in both) and the finite values agree within tolerance. Each NaN probe asserts the oracle really is NaN somewhere, so the comparison cannot pass vacuously. No divergence found, so no kernel changes. `selective_scan_backward_nan` does the same for the Mamba-1 `SelectiveScanBackwardPlan` (no z gate, D skip, bias or softplus; batch 2, 4 channels, d_state 4, 16 steps; probes NaN in dy, u, B, C, delta, A; e.g. a NaN in A gives 125 NaN cells in both): the pattern matches the oracle cell for cell in f32, f16 and bf16, and the control matches on values. No divergence. Not probed: `+inf` inputs, other shapes (chunk sizes, d_state beyond 4), the optional z gate, D skip, delta bias and softplus paths.

**FlashAttention-2 audit.** `crates/baracuda-kernels/tests/fa2_nan_audit.rs` forces the FA2 backend (f16 and bf16) and checks the E0k table on a real device (RTX 4070, sm89): a NaN query row gives that row NaN only; a NaN attended key or value poisons every row that attends it (per kv head under GQA); a `+inf` score gives a NaN row, except under a softcap, where the score is bounded and the row stays finite; a fully masked row (causal with Q > K, or outside a sliding window) is exactly zero. Measured: MHA D=64, GQA (4 q heads, 2 kv heads), D=128 and D=256, sliding window (non-causal and causal), softcap 30, and their combination: 20 of 20 pass, no divergence from the bespoke kernels. A NaN value at a position a row does not attend DOES reach that row on FA2 (UNSPECIFIED by contract, printed not asserted). UNMEASURED: ALiBi, head dims 32/96/192, shapes with Q*K >= 1M, and the FA2 backward.

**Naive attention backward.** `attention_sdpa_backward_nan` runs the naive `baracuda_kernels_sdpa_{f32,f16,bf16}_run` then `..._backward_run` (`SdpaBackwardPlan`, which consumes the SAVED attention matrix) against the same f64 IEEE oracle as the flash backward test (finite control, then NaN in Q, K, V, dO and a `+inf` Q component): the NaN pattern of dQ, dK and dV matches cell for cell in f32, f16 and bf16 (6 of 6 pass), so the naive kernel does NOT have the lse conflation fixed in alpha.210 for the flash kernel. A fully masked row is not probed there (no `lse`; the saved attention row is zeros).

**FA2 backward (`fa2_backward_nan`, f16, d=64, device-tests + fa2_backward).** A NaN or `+inf` query row gives all-NaN `dV` and a NaN `dQ` row, a finite control gives finite gradients (3 of 3 as torch). A NaN KEY differs: `dQ` and `dK` are all NaN but `dV` is NaN only in the poisoned key's row (64 of 8192 elements), where torch gives NaN for every `dV` (row softmax over a NaN score is all NaN). KNOWN DIVERGENCE from the torch convention, measured with f16, d=64, h=1, 128x128, non-causal on the device (RTX 4070, sm_89) against the vendored FA2 v2.8.3 (commit 060c9188). Cause read from vendor `softmax.h`, NOT mutant-isolated (a hypothesis until a mutant on that vendored line shows it): the FA2 forward stores `lse = +inf` for a row whose softmax sum is NaN (`softmax.h`: `sum == 0 || sum != sum`), so the backward's `exp(s * scale - lse)` is 0 for every finite score and NaN only for the NaN score. The NaN still reaches the caller through `dQ` and `dK`. `nan_key_fa2_backward_dv_is_nan_only_for_the_poisoned_key` pins today's behaviour (it was red against the torch expectation, `dV` all NaN, at 64 of 8192). Fuel binds no flash backward; not fixed here (vendored upstream code).
